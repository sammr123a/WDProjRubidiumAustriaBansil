Introduction to Rap

The 1990s were one of the defining eras of rap music. During this decade, rap grew more powerful, more expressive, and more influential. Artists created unforgettable lyrics that captured the realities and the attitudes of the time. Many new styles also rose such as gangsta rap, conscious rap, and alternative rap, each showcasing their own sound and message. The decade was also fueled by the East and West coast rivalry, shaping and influencing the rap scene.

East Coast vs. West Coast

The East Coast vs. West Coast rivalry was a major conflict in 1990s hip-hop, centered mainly between New York–based artists like The Notorious B.I.G. and Bad Boy Records, and Los Angeles artists like Tupac Shakur and Death Row Records. What began as competition for influence and recognition in rap quickly turned into a heated feud fueled by diss tracks, media hype, and personal tensions between artists and record labels. The rivalry shaped the sound and culture of 90s rap, pushing both coasts to produce some of their most iconic music. However, it also escalated into real violence, ultimately contributing to the tragic deaths of both Tupac and Biggie, which shocked the music world and highlighted the dangerous consequences of the feud.

Resolution

In the end, the East vs. West coast rivalry slowly faded after the deaths of Tupac and Biggie. Nonetheless, this conflict left a permanent mark on hip-hop, leaving a legacy of powerful storytelling and bold creativity. its impact continues to echo today, serving as a reminder of both the risks and artistic breakthroughs that come from intense creative competition.
